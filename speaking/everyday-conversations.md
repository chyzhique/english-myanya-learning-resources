# Everyday Conversations: English Conversation for Beginners

English conversation for beginners fails when you only study word lists. You need short, reusable dialogues — neighbour, shop, work kitchen — then English speaking practice that forces your voice into the room.

These three scenes lean British English. Keep them short. Change names and details to match your life.

## Dialogue 1: Neighbour

**A:** You alright?  
**B:** Yeah, not bad. You?  
**A:** Surviving. Busy week.  
**B:** Same. Fancy a coffee sometime?  
**A:** Yeah, go on then. Tomorrow after work?  
**B:** Works for me. See you then.

**Practice:** Swap *coffee* for *tea* or *a walk*. Record both parts.

## Dialogue 2: Shop

**A:** Hiya — just this, please.  
**B:** Anything else?  
**A:** No, that's it. Have you got a bag?  
**B:** Yeah. Card or cash?  
**A:** Card, please.  
**B:** There you go.  
**A:** Cheers.

**Practice:** Add one line asking where something is. Keep answers short.

## Dialogue 3: Work kitchen

**A:** Is this milk still alright?  
**B:** Should be — I opened it yesterday.  
**A:** Mind if I grab a mug?  
**B:** Go for it. Fancy a biscuit?  
**A:** Go on then. Cheers.  
**B:** I'll leave you to it — meeting in five.

**Practice:** Change the snack and the reason to leave. Say it without looking.

## Practice tips

1. Learn one dialogue a day for three days. Day four: mix lines.
2. Shadow each scene once for English pronunciation practice, then speak without the text.
3. Record 60 seconds. Fix two issues only (past tense, word stress, ending sounds).
4. Reuse everyday English phrases from the [everyday English](../vocabulary/everyday-english.md) list inside these scenes.
