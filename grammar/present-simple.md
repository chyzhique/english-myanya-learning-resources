# Present Simple: Textbook vs Street

The present simple uses the base verb (I work) or *-s/-es* with he/she/it (she works). Textbooks teach it for habits and facts. Real speech uses it for schedules, opinions, and quiet complaints — especially in British English.

## Textbook

- I work in an office.
- She lives in London.
- They don't eat meat.

Clear. Correct. A bit flat if that is all you say.

## Street (British English)

Use the same form for routines, likes, and "how things usually go" — the language of kitchen tables and train platforms.

### Three real-world examples

1. **"I usually get the 8.12."**  
   Habit plus schedule. Natural British English for commuting talk. Present simple for the pattern of your life.

2. **"He doesn't fancy pubs much."**  
   Soft preference. *Fancy* keeps the line conversational. Useful English phrases for real life when someone asks about plans.

3. **"Do you take milk?"**  
   Everyday offer at tea time. Short. Polite. The kind of English practice you need more than essay sentences.

## Quick contrast

| Textbook feel | Street feel |
| --- | --- |
| I drink coffee every morning. | I have a coffee on the way in. |
| She does not like football. | She's not that into football. |
| Do you live near here? | Do you live round here? |
