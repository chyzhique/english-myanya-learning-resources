# Can / Can't: Ability, Permission, Requests

`Can` and `can't` cover ability (I can swim), permission (Can I sit here?), and requests (Can you open the window?). Textbooks list the three uses. Street English mixes them in one busy day — especially in British English.

## Textbook

- I can speak English.
- You can't park here.
- Can you help me?

Correct. Useful. Still a bit stiff in some situations.

## Street (British English)

Same forms. Softer rhythm. Often paired with *please*, *sorry*, or a short reason.

### Three real-world examples

1. **"Can I borrow a pen for a sec?"**  
   Permission plus request. Casual British English. Everyday English phrases like this keep English practice tied to real rooms.

2. **"I can't make it tonight — something's come up."**  
   Ability/availability. Soft refusal without a long story. Useful English conversation when plans change.

3. **"Can you just hold this a minute?"**  
   Request under pressure. Short. Human. The line you need in a shop queue or a kitchen.

## Quick contrast

| Textbook feel | Street feel |
| --- | --- |
| Can you help me, please? | Can you give us a hand? |
| I cannot come tomorrow. | I can't do tomorrow, sorry. |
| Can I open the window? | Mind if I open the window? |

## Practice tip

Write three ability lines, three permission lines, three requests about *your* week. Say them aloud. Swap one formal line for a street form. That is English grammar for English speaking practice.
