# Past Simple: Textbook vs Street

The past simple uses regular *-ed* forms (I walked) and irregular forms (I went, I saw, I bought). Textbooks drill timelines. Real speech uses it to tell what happened — quickly, with feeling, and without a timeline chart.

## Textbook

- I visited my friend yesterday.
- She finished work at six.
- They didn't catch the bus.

Clear. Correct. Often too neat for how people actually talk.

## Street (British English)

Past simple carries stories about your day, excuses, and small dramas. British English learners need the sound of endings (*worked*, *missed*) as much as the spelling.

### Three real-world examples

1. **"I missed the train by two minutes."**  
   Classic complaint. Past simple for a finished event. Useful English conversation fuel after work.

2. **"We popped into the shop on the way home."**  
   *Popped into* is casual British English. Same grammar, warmer tone than "We entered the shop."

3. **"Did you get my message?"**  
   Everyday check-in. Short. Direct. The kind of English phrases for real life you will use all week.

## Quick contrast

| Textbook feel | Street feel |
| --- | --- |
| I went to the cinema last night. | I went out to the pictures last night. |
| She did not like the film. | She wasn't keen on it. |
| Did you buy milk? | Did you grab any milk? |
