# Present Continuous: Textbook vs Street

The present continuous is `am/is/are + verb-ing`. Textbooks use it for actions happening now. Real speech uses it for plans, complaints, and soft invitations too — especially in British English.

## Textbook

- I am reading a book.
- She is working at the moment.
- They are waiting for the bus.

Clear. Correct. A bit stiff if that is all you say.

## Street (British English)

Use the same form when something is temporary, arranged, or in progress in your life — not only when you are mid-action.

### Three real-world examples

1. **"Are you coming to the pub?"**  
   Not a grammar drill. A Friday invitation. Present continuous for a near-future plan that feels already in motion.

2. **"I'm just nipping to the shop."**  
   Temporary action. Casual British English. You would not write this in a formal email, but you will hear it all week in everyday English.

3. **"She's not answering — she's driving."**  
   Explains why something is not happening right now. Useful in English phrases for real life: calls, texts, quick excuses that sound natural.

## Quick contrast

| Textbook feel | Street feel |
| --- | --- |
| I am studying English. | I'm working on my English this month. |
| Are you going to the cinema? | Are you coming out later? |
| He is cooking dinner. | He's doing dinner now — call back in ten. |
