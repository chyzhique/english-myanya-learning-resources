# A2 English: Longer Turns, Past Forms, Everyday Tasks

You can greet people and ask simple questions. A2 is the step where English lessons for beginners stretch into real errands and short stories. You need past forms, shop and travel talk, and the habit of speaking in full turns — not one-word answers.

A2 is not advanced grammar. It is control under pressure: ordering food, explaining a delay, telling what happened yesterday. Keep British English spelling if Europe is your target.

## Five actionable steps

1. **Own the past simple for your week.** Drill: I went, I saw, I bought, I missed, I called. Write five true sentences about the last seven days. Say them aloud. English grammar sticks when it describes your life, not a textbook character.
2. **Build task dialogues.** Shop, pharmacy, train station, café. Write both sides. Practise English conversation for beginners until the replies feel automatic. Everyday English phrases beat long vocabulary lists.
3. **Stretch answers to three sentences.** When someone asks "How was your weekend?", do not stop at "Fine." Add where, what, and one detail. That is English speaking practice that sounds human.
4. **Listen for past markers.** In a short British English clip, note *yesterday*, *last*, *ago*, and *-ed* endings. Shadow one minute. English pronunciation practice here is noticing what you already half-know.
5. **Keep a tiny English study plan.** Fifteen minutes grammar or phrases. Ten minutes speaking. Five minutes review. Same time each day. Self-paced English course logic without opening ten tabs.

## Weekly shape

Monday–Wednesday: past forms + one task dialogue. Thursday–Friday: record a 90-second story about your week. Saturday: review travel or shop phrases. Sunday: one longer beginner listen. That is an English learning path you can hold after work.
