# Restaurant English: Café and Table Phrases

Textbook restaurant English is stiff. Real cafés and pubs move fast. You need short lines for ordering, asking, and paying — English phrases for real life, not menu essays. British English lean below.

## 10 restaurant / café phrases for real life

1. **Table for two, please.**  
   Textbook: We would like a table for two people.  
   Street: First line at the door. Clear English practice for busy evenings.

2. **Have you got a table outside?**  
   Textbook: Is there outdoor seating available?  
   Street: Natural British English ask on a warm day.

3. **I'll have the…, please.**  
   Textbook: I would like to order the…  
   Street: Standard order line. Short. Human.

4. **What's in the…?**  
   Textbook: What are the ingredients of…?  
   Street: Quick check for allergies or picky eaters.

5. **Could we have a bit longer?**  
   Textbook: We need more time to decide.  
   Street: Soft delay when the waiter returns early.

6. **Is the bill all right to split?**  
   Textbook: Can we pay separately?  
   Street: Common British English wording at the end of a meal.

7. **Just a tap, please. / Card, please.**  
   Textbook: I will pay by card.  
   Street: Contactless reality. Everyday English phrases staff hear all day.

8. **Could I get this without…?**  
   Textbook: Please remove … from my order.  
   Street: Clear change request without a speech.

9. **We're ready to order when you are.**  
   Textbook: We are ready to order now.  
   Street: Polite signal. Good English conversation manners.

10. **That was lovely, thanks.**  
    Textbook: The meal was very good. Thank you.  
    Street: Warm exit line. British English understatement included.

## How to train these

- Practise ordering your real usual meal aloud.
- Add one complaint line and one praise line.
- Role-play both customer and staff for sixty seconds.
- Record once for English speaking practice; fix two sounds.
