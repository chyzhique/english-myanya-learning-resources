# Travel English: Phrases for Real Life

Textbook travel English sounds like a brochure. You need lines for platforms, delays, and asking strangers without sounding robotic. Below are ten travel phrases for real life, with a textbook contrast. British English lean for Europe routes and UK stations.

## 10 travel phrases for real life

1. **Is this the right platform for…?**  
   Textbook: Which platform do I need?  
   Street: Quick check before you commit. Calm English phrases for real life under time pressure.

2. **Does this stop at…?**  
   Textbook: Does this train stop in…?  
   Street: Shorter. Same meaning. Useful English practice on a crowded carriage.

3. **Sorry — is anyone sitting here?**  
   Textbook: Is this seat free?  
   Street: Soft opener. British English politeness that still gets an answer.

4. **How long does it take to get to…?**  
   Textbook: What is the journey time to…?  
   Street: Natural question for buses, tubes, and taxis.

5. **I've missed my connection.**  
   Textbook: I did not catch my connecting train.  
   Street: Clear problem statement at the information desk.

6. **Where do I change for…?**  
   Textbook: Where should I change trains for…?  
   Street: Short. Direct. The line you actually say.

7. **Is there a delay on this service?**  
   Textbook: Is the train late?  
   Street: Station language. Common British English wording.

8. **Could you speak a bit more slowly, please?**  
   Textbook: Please speak slowly.  
   Street: Polite and usable when announcements or staff go fast.

9. **I'm just looking for the way out.**  
   Textbook: How do I exit the station?  
   Street: Soft ask when you are lost but not in crisis.

10. **Single to…, please. / Return to…, please.**  
    Textbook: I would like a one-way / round-trip ticket to…  
    Street: Ticket-window British English. Learn both forms.

## How to train these

- Pick five phrases before a trip or a practice week.
- Write one situation for each (platform, desk, taxi).
- Say the line, then the likely reply.
- Record a 60-second role-play for English pronunciation practice.
