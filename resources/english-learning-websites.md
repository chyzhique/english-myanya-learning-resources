# English Learning Websites: Use Free Sites Without Drowning

Free English learning websites are endless. That is the problem. You open five tabs: grammar, videos, worksheets, a forum, a dictionary. Two hours later you have bookmarks and no English practice.

This page is a curated approach — how to use the open web without drowning. This repository is the map. Sites are fuel.

## The drowning pattern

1. Search "learn English" or "English for beginners".
2. Open everything that looks free.
3. Switch topics every ten minutes.
4. Close the laptop tired and unsure what improved.

That is not an English learning path. That is browsing.

## A simple rule set

1. **One job per session.** Grammar hole, or vocabulary set, or listening — not all three.
2. **One main site + this hub.** Use this repo to decide *what* to study. Use one website to *do* the drill.
3. **Speak or write before you leave.** Silent reading alone is weak English self study.
4. **Cap the tabs.** Three maximum. Close the rest.
5. **Reuse for a week.** Same phrases, same forms. Depth beats novelty.

## What to look for on free sites

- Clear English lessons for beginners with examples you can say aloud
- British English audio if that is your target
- Short texts with everyday English phrases, not only exam prose
- Downloadable or copyable lines you can put in a notebook

Skip comment wars and infinite "related lesson" sidebars. They steal the session.
