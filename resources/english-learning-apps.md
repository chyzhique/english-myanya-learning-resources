# English Learning Apps: What They Are Good For (and Not)

English learning apps can help. They can also waste your evening. This page is an honest frame for choosing English practice tools — not a ranked ad list, and not a takedown of named products.

**A course. Not a chat.** Keep that filter when you install anything.

## What apps are often good for

- Short daily touchpoints when you have ten minutes on a commute
- Drilling fixed forms: irregular verbs, spelling, listening micro-tasks
- Keeping a simple English study plan visible on your phone
- Recording yourself if the app supports speaking checks with a clear task

Useful English practice starts with a task you can finish. Apps that give you a prompt and a finish line help more than apps that only keep you scrolling.

## What apps are often bad for

- **Streak apps** that reward opening the app more than speaking clearly
- **Open chat** that never tells you what to study next — busy, not structured
- Random lessons with no English learning path from your level onward
- Endless vocab cards with no English phrases for real life attached to a scene

If you leave a session unable to say what you practised, the tool failed you — even if the streak grew.

## Criteria for choosing an English practice app

1. **Does it force production?** Speaking or writing you create, not only taps.
2. **Is there a map?** Levels, goals, or an English learning path you can follow (A0 to C1 English sketch, not chaos).
3. **Can you reuse phrases?** Everyday English phrases and dialogues beat isolated words.
4. **Is feedback specific?** Two clear fixes beat a vague score.
5. **Does it fit adults?** English lessons for beginners should respect kitchen-table learners — no babyish noise, no hype.
6. **British English option?** Spelling and listening matter if Europe or the UK is your target.
7. **Will it still help at B1?** Or does it trap you in A1 loops?
